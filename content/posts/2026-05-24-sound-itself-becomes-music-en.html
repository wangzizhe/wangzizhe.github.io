I accidentally came across Ryuichi Sakamoto’s late work "solari", and then listened through the entire "async" album. I found it fascinating. As I listened more carefully, I suddenly found myself crying for no obvious reason. Previously, whenever music made me cry, it was usually because of some emotion it evoked. But listening to this album felt different. There was no particular emotion attached to it. Somehow, I was simply crying because of the music itself, and I could not stop. These pieces also feel incredibly visual. When listening to "solari", for example, I often feel as if I am floating somewhere in space, weightless.

Many years ago, I would have been completely incapable of appreciating this album or this kind of music. I probably would have dismissed it as noise and questioned whether the composer had lost their mind. But after years of systematic musical training and consciously exposing myself to modern classical music, I realized that I have gradually reached a point where I can genuinely appreciate this kind of music.

Looking back, my relationship with music has gone through several stages. At first, I cared mostly about melody. After I started studying classical piano seriously, I became interested in structure and layers. Eventually, through Chopin, I became fascinated by harmony. Through Debussy, I started paying attention to color. Then this album came along, and for the first time, I realized I had started paying attention to sound itself.

Many tracks on this album barely have recognizable melodies or harmonic movement. In some sense, they have already moved beyond what we traditionally think of as music, and yet the sounds themselves are fascinating. Several pieces were apparently created using a piano that had been completely damaged by seawater after the 2011 tsunami. I honestly cannot hear exactly where those sounds are, but after asking AI about it, I learned that Sakamoto would stretch out the overtones of piano sounds and use electronic processing to layer them underneath as atmosphere. Even after learning this, I still cannot really hear it, but somehow those sounds became even more interesting afterward.

I first learned about Ryuichi Sakamoto back in 2024, when I accidentally came across the documentary "Coda". The documentary captured much of the process behind making this album. Looking back now, maybe that was when I slowly started realizing that music could simply be sound itself.

Recently, I have also started consciously paying attention to sounds in nature. Walking outside, I find myself listening to traffic and city noise. Lying on the grass, I pay attention to leaves moving in the wind. But honestly, I still do not know why "async" makes me cry. I still cannot explain it. It does not remind me of any specific memories, nor does it correspond to any clear emotion. For the first time, I realized that some sounds enter your ears, bypass your habitual ways of understanding, and arrive somewhere that even you cannot quite explain.
