Over the past four weeks, I've been meeting with one investor each week, and it's been an incredibly insightful experience. There are still many things I haven't fully thought through, so every meeting is an opportunity to learn and get valuable feedback. Their advice and questions often give me new perspectives and make me think about things I hadn't considered before.

One question particularly stuck with me: Does improving efficiency necessarily reduce costs and ultimately make a business more profitable? I had always taken this for granted, but I've come to realize that it's not true in every scenario. In the age of large language models, the marginal cost of serving additional users is no longer close to zero. With personal agents, both user growth and increased usage drive up costs. In coding, the cost of tokens can sometimes even exceed the cost of having a human do the same task. So ultimately, the economics need to make sense. The answer itself is one thing, but how we approach the question may be even more important. I hadn't thought about this before and didn't quite know how to answer at the time. Spending some time thinking it through afterward was quite eye-opening.

I've always tended to approach problems from a technical perspective. Over the past month, I've increasingly learned to think from a business perspective, as well as from the perspective of the product and its users. I've also gained some practical experience, such as how to prepare for investor meetings, what to discuss, and how to approach those conversations. I've met some genuinely good people, and I've taken something valuable away from every conversation.

Of course, this shift in perspective has also brought new challenges. But I think these challenges are necessary. We need to keep stepping outside our comfort zones and challenging ourselves. Over the past week, I've spent a lot of time thinking through these questions, with quite a bit of self-doubt along the way. But through this process of questioning myself and looking for answers, I've gained much more clarity on many things.
