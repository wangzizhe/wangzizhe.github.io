My grandma passed away yesterday. She was 94, and she went peacefully.

On Thursday morning, my mom did something very unusual. She texted me at 6:30 asking if I was awake. Right away, I had a bad feeling. Then she told me to wash up and video call my grandma. At that point, I had basically guessed what was going on. Still, I asked, “What happened?” She said, “It’s not looking good.” Then she added, “She might not even recognize you anymore.”

I immediately put down my toothbrush, rinsed my mouth, and video called my mom. On the screen, my grandma was lying there. It wasn’t quite what I had imagined. She looked like she was just sleeping, very gentle, with a calm expression, almost like a quiet little Buddha statue. I couldn’t tell if she was unconscious or had fallen ill. My mom told me to call her, so I called out “Grandma” a few times. My mom also told my grandma that I was on the phone. Then my grandma slowly opened her right eye and murmured my childhood nickname. My mom asked if she knew who I was, and she could still say it. Then she asked what I called her, and she knew it was “Grandma.” Her speech was already a bit slurred, but she still remembered me. My mom was surprised, because my grandma no longer recognized her, and didn’t recognize my aunt either. She even thought my aunt was her own mother. My mom told me that she had started saying things that didn’t make sense, often talking to herself, as if she had entered some kind of hallucination.

Seeing her like that, I knew she was at the end of her life. The tears came all at once, and I couldn’t stop them. So many memories from the past started playing in my mind, one after another, like scenes in a film.

I hung up, washed my face, and sat down for breakfast. By then my girlfriend had gotten up too. She saw me sitting there, quietly eating and barely speaking, and asked, “Why do you seem unhappy?” I still didn’t say anything, because I felt that the moment I spoke, the tears would come. Then she walked over and stood beside me, gently stroking my head, and asked, “What’s wrong?” I held back my tears, but I knew that if I opened my mouth, they would start to fall. She kept gently stroking my head like that. In the end, I couldn’t hold it in anymore, and I started crying. She saw me wiping my tears and seemed a bit startled, quickly asking what happened. “Did something come to mind?” I told her that my grandma might be leaving soon. She sat down with me, talked to me, comforted me, and stayed with me. Gradually, I began to feel a little better.

On Friday morning, as soon as I woke up, I video called my mom and asked how my grandma was doing. She said they had taken her to the hospital and had started preparing for what was coming. By then, my grandma was completely unresponsive. I thought to myself that I was lucky I talked to her the day before. Otherwise, I might never have gotten the chance again. I also understood that her body was gradually shutting down. Her brain had mostly stopped, but her body was still running on instinct. Her heart was beating hard, and her breathing was using the last of its strength. It felt like her soul had already gone somewhere else, and only her body was still holding on to the last trace of life. I knew that in this state, it was probably just a matter of hours, maybe a day or two at most. I wasn’t even sure if I would still be able to see her the next day. My mom said she would let me see her again, but deep down I already had a feeling.

On Saturday morning, I video called my mom again as soon as I woke up. The moment she picked up, she said, “Your grandma has passed. 8:53 this morning. She was just a month away from her birthday.” My aunt added, “She went very peacefully.”

The last elder in our family is gone. My mom was devastated. She looked like she had lost her soul, crying nonstop. I understand her. The one person in this world who loved her unconditionally is gone.

There is a line from an essay by Bi Shumin that says, “When your parents are still alive, you still have somewhere to return to. When they are gone, life becomes a one-way journey to its end.” It means that when your parents are around, home is a refuge, a place you can always go back to. But once they are gone, you lose that deepest sense of where you came from, and life begins to feel like a one-way journey.

I am not at my parents’ age yet, and I have not truly experienced what it is like to lose them. But I understand the weight of that sentence.

I often think about it. Someone like me who is always exploring my limits, trying different things, never afraid of failure, wanting to live a life that is truly my own. Where does that confidence come from? It is actually very simple. I know that no matter what happens, whether I end up with nothing, fail completely, or even get abandoned by the whole world, there will always be a place waiting for me. There will always be someone who accepts me and loves me unconditionally.

When I was a kid, my family, my grandparents on my dad’s side, and my grandparents on my mom’s side all lived in the same compound, just a few minutes’ walk from each other. Back in those days, employers assigned housing, so everyone lived close together. Within five minutes, I could walk from our place to either of my grandparents’ homes. I was a very playful kid. Whenever I got tired, thirsty, or hungry, I could just knock on my grandparents’ door, and someone would always open it for me. I could even reach in from outside and open the door to my grandma’s place on my mom’s side myself. Later, I realized that not many of my classmates and friends had that kind of childhood. Even among my cousins, I was the only one who had this experience. Whenever my parents were not home, I could always go to my grandparents. During school breaks, I never had to worry about being taken care of.

That is why I was especially close to them. I remember the day my grandma on my dad’s side passed away. I felt like I had lost my soul. The tears would not stop, and scenes from my childhood kept replaying in my mind. After that, I often dreamed about them. Sometimes there would even be dreams within dreams. In the deeper dream, I knew they had already passed away. But when I woke up into the outer dream, they were still alive. I would feel confused. Weren’t they gone? Then I would realize it was just a dream, that they were still here. But when I finally woke up into reality, it would take me a long time to figure out what was real. And when I realized that in reality they were truly gone, that sense of loss and sadness would come rushing back all over again.

Now, the last elder in our family, my grandma on my mom’s side, is gone too. Unlike the others, she passed in the most peaceful way. It was simply old age, without pain or suffering. Her children were by her side when she left. From her perspective, I think that is a kind of completeness. Sometimes I think that when my time comes, I hope I can leave this world just as quietly.

I am grateful for the unconditional love my grandparents gave me. I grew up surrounded by love, and that makes me feel deeply fulfilled. That love is the foundation of my life. It is what allows me to face any hardship without fear.

I often think about those days from my childhood. The compound at 40 Dexing East Street, where I grew up. From the balcony at home, I could see my grandma on my dad’s side cooking in her kitchen. When I was in elementary school, every day after school, she would be waiting for me at the door, helping me carry my schoolbag and buying me fried dough cakes. Every summer, I would stay at her place, messing around and teasing my cousin. Every time I saw my grandma on my mom’s side, she was always smiling, warm and gentle, with that kind of easygoing warmth. Every time I left her place, she would follow me out and watch me until I walked past the corner of that small road, only then turning back. At her table, she would keep putting meat into my bowl, again and again, and there was that braised eggplant, the best I have ever had.

What a beautiful childhood it was.

I will always remember you. You will always live in my heart.
