A Small Goal

My PhD defense is just around the corner. Honestly, as someone who was never particularly good at school growing up, this is something I never would have dared to imagine as a kid.

The biggest change in me over the past few years is probably that I’ve become much more willing to dream big. When I was younger, I always thought of myself as just an ordinary person. I never believed I could do anything especially remarkable. But as I got older, I saw more of the world, met more people, and tried more things. Some of those things, after years of persistence, actually started to go pretty well. A few of them even seemed impossible in other people’s eyes. The most obvious example is probably the piano. I started from scratch as an adult, and somehow made it to where I am today. Little by little, I became more willing to imagine what else might be possible. To me, the meaning of life is in the experience of it. A lot of the things I want to do may never actually work out, but the experience itself is one of the most important parts of being alive. If it works, that’s amazing. If it doesn’t, that’s perfectly normal too. Whenever you seriously work toward something, you almost always gain something unexpected along the way. So in my mind, having a goal and genuinely committing yourself to it is already a form of success.

Right now, I have a few goals. I want to build a company that has a major impact on an industry. I want it to achieve positive cash flow without relying on outside funding. And I want to “retire” within the next two years. Or, more accurately, I want to become financially independent, so I no longer have to work just to make a living.

But I’ve also thought about this: if I retired today, I would probably wake up tomorrow and keep doing exactly what I’m doing now, because it’s exactly what I’m passionate about. 99% of startups fail. Maybe I have a 1% chance of surviving, and maybe a 0.00001% chance of one day ringing the opening bell. But living with that possibility is a strangely wonderful experience. I’ve also wondered what it would feel like to try this at fifty or sixty. Or what I would be like if I had already achieved financial freedom and no longer had the courage to go all in. That’s why I think the state I’m in right now is actually a very good one. I may have nothing, but somehow, I feel like I already have everything I need.

So, haha, let me give it a try and see whether I can achieve this small goal. Most likely, I won’t. But what if I do?
