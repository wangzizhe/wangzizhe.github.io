Ten Years Later, I Finally Begin Writing Music

Today, I officially begin learning composition. In truth, this idea has been sitting quietly in my mind for a full ten years.

About ten years ago, I first became interested in music. At that time, I seriously considered the possibility that I might learn composition. But after looking into it briefly, I was struck by how complex it seemed. I quickly realized that if I ever wanted to talk about composing, the first thing I needed to do was simply step through the door into the world of music.

So I began thinking about two questions:
First, was my enthusiasm for music just a passing interest, or something I could sustain for many years?
Second, if I truly wanted to enter the world of music, what would be the best point of entry? These questions stayed with me, and I thought about them on and off for several years.

Gradually, I became more and more certain that this was not just a fleeting impulse. I genuinely wanted to study music seriously. After a great deal of research and reflection, I came to feel that the piano might be the most suitable instrument for me to enter the world of music. So I decided to learn the piano.

For the first year and a half, I taught myself. But over time I realized that self-teaching made it very difficult to truly improve, because there were many problems I simply could not recognize on my own. So I decided to find a teacher and study properly. In the summer of 2020, I found my current piano teacher, and from that moment I began studying piano in a systematic way. To my surprise, that study has now lasted almost six years.

Over these six years, I have encountered music from many different periods and styles. Among them, the music of two composers has fascinated me deeply. The first is Chopin. I love his melodies and harmonies, the singing quality of his melodic lines and the richness of his emotional expression always draw me in completely. The other is Debussy. The first time I truly studied one of Debussy's works, it felt like a sudden revelation. His harmonies and tonal colors opened an entirely new world for me. Music was no longer just the development of melody, but something more like light, color, and air flowing through space.

During these years I also began attending concerts more frequently. In the course of this process, I gradually discovered something interesting: I do not actually feel a strong connection with symphonic music. In May 2025, I attended a concert by Hiromi Uehara. That performance was mind-blowing. Many of the harmonies in her music made me marvel again and again. I had never realized the piano could produce sounds like that. The feeling was very similar to the first time I encountered Debussy. In the second half of 2025, I also happened to come across the music of Genshin Impact. Those pieces accompanied me through much of that period. Later I began looking into its composer, Yu-Peng Chen, and discovered that I truly love his music.

Gradually, I came to realize something about my own taste. I am deeply drawn to a kind of music whose structure may not be extremely complex, but whose layers are remarkably rich.

During this process, I also began to remember that thought from ten years ago. Looking back now, I have already stepped through the door into the world of music. I have developed a very clear musical aesthetic, and I have begun to truly analyze music itself, rather than focusing only on performance technique. Since 2025, I have started trying to understand music more deeply, including harmony, structure, and the layers within sound.

It feels as if everything has slowly fallen into place. So once again I remembered that thought from ten years ago. Perhaps now is the moment to realize it.

So, starting today.

On March 15, 2026, I officially begin my journey in composition.

I hope that one day I will be able to express myself through music.
