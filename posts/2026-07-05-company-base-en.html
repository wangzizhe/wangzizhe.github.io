Where Should I Base My Company?

Lately, I have been thinking a lot about one question: if my startup really succeeds, where should I base the company? Germany, Hong Kong, and Singapore all make sense in different ways, but none of them feels like the obvious answer. Germany is closest to the life I have now, yet over the past few years I have become increasingly aware of the gap between Germany, and Europe more broadly, and China and the United States in terms of startup culture, access to capital, and the concentration of top technology talent. If my long-term ambition is to build an AI lab operating at the frontier, I am not convinced that Germany or Europe can provide the environment or talent density needed to make that possible.

For now, I am setting the United States aside because the geopolitical situation is simply too complicated. China, on the other hand, could offer much greater room for growth if the company succeeds. It has a faster pace of execution, a deeper pool of engineering talent, and a more developed industrial and startup ecosystem. Singapore has also become increasingly popular with Chinese founders, partly because it offers a way to reduce some of the uncertainty created by geopolitics. But Singapore comes with a very practical problem: both living and operating costs are high. Moving there before the company has truly gained traction, simply to claim the label of an international headquarters, would feel like putting the structure before the business. A Singapore headquarters would only make sense once the company has meaningful revenue or funding and can genuinely support a team and management presence there.

Seen this way, Germany offers me a relatively stable starting point. China offers speed, talent density, and a more dynamic startup environment. Singapore offers international reach and the ability to connect different markets. I still have no idea where I will actually be living a few years from now. This is the first time in my life that my future location feels completely open. For most of my life, the next step was usually clear. Now it is not. I do not see that as confusion. I see it as possibility.

For now, the more sensible order is probably to build the product, find customers who are genuinely willing to pay, and then see which market, talent pool, investors, and way of life begin to shape the company. If European industrial customers become the core of the business, Germany may still be the most natural place to start. If the company needs faster execution, a denser engineering talent pool, and a more energetic startup ecosystem, China may become more attractive. If the business becomes genuinely global and the company can support an international management team, Singapore may finally become the right home. I do not need to decide today where I ultimately belong. The more important thing is to build something real first, then let the customers, the team, and the opportunities that actually emerge show me where the next chapter should begin.
