What We Get Wrong About Risk

I recently watched a short clip of Sam Altman, and one point really stuck with me: most people are wrong about what is actually risky.

https://youtu.be/dKGQMpVS8v4

For many people, embarrassment, criticism, temporarily losing income, or taking a detour in life are seen as high-risk, while seemingly stable and predictable choices are taken for granted as low-risk. But sometimes, the opposite is true. Especially when we're young, with few responsibilities and little to lose, we have much more room to make mistakes than we think. The real risk is not trying and failing, but looking back years later and realizing that we never truly pursued what we wanted because we were afraid of failure, discomfort, or uncertainty.

By overestimating short-term risks and underestimating the long-term cost of doing nothing, many people stay in a comfort zone where they feel productive: discussing ideas, making plans, attending events, and exploring different things without going deep into any of them. Yet they never really build anything or fully commit to something they believe in. Thinking and preparation are important, but without action, they can easily become a way of making ourselves feel better.

What truly moves us forward is not talking about ideas or thinking about them, but understanding the risks, taking action on what we believe in, and accepting the uncertainty that comes with it. This is not about taking risks blindly, but about rethinking what risk really means. Sometimes, what's more concerning than making the wrong choice is never making a choice at all because we're afraid of getting it wrong.

For me, the biggest regret in life is not trying and failing, but having the chance to try and choosing not to because of fear or doubt. So whenever I'm deciding whether to do something, no matter how many practical factors I need to consider, it ultimately comes down to one question: If I don't do this now, will I regret it years from now?
