In Memory of Jensen

Deep learning has been around for about 40 years, but for a long time, it was a very niche field. Computer science students might have learned about it in class, but it was such a minor topic that professors rarely spent much time on it. Everything changed in 2012, when AlexNet, trained on GPUs, shocked the world with its performance in the ImageNet competition. That marked the beginning of the deep learning era. Then came the boom in computer vision, Transformers, large language models, and the generative AI revolution we're seeing today. Without CUDA, it's hard to imagine any of this happening, let alone the incredible AI revolution we're living through. And without Jensen's persistence, CUDA might never have existed, and neither would much of what we see today.

Today, training large language models has largely become a race for computing power. The more computing power you have, the better your chances of leading in AI. In a sense, Jensen didn't just build NVIDIA into a great company. He also helped drive the deep learning revolution and played a major role in making the US the world's leader in AI.

And yet, at a time like this, one shocking accusation after another has come out, without any solid evidence to back them up. I can't help but wonder if Jensen has become a victim of political infighting. I even suspect that some kind of deal may have been made. If he hadn't admitted to anything or stepped down, perhaps more accusations would have followed until he finally gave in. The board was in a difficult position. Supporting him could have put the entire company at greater risk, but letting him go meant losing the heart and soul of NVIDIA. In the end, it seems Jensen was left with no choice but to sacrifice himself in this political battle.

Tragic!
