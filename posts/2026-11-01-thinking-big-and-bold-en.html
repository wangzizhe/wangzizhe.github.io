I Realized I Haven't Been Thinking Big or Bold Enough

Over the past two weeks, I've applied to ten investment-backed startup accelerators: Schub, EF (Entrepreneurs First), Heartfelt, EWOR, Antler, Techstars, Z Fellows, Alchemist, a16z, and YC (Y Combinator). Many of these programs bring founders to San Francisco for 8–12 weeks of intense work: meeting like-minded people, building startups, and preparing to pitch investors. The odds of getting accepted are incredibly low, often somewhere between 0.1% and 0.01%, or even lower. And the application process itself takes a surprising amount of time and energy. There are tons of questions to answer, some of them pretty unconventional (or just plain weird), and the answers often require a lot of thinking and rewriting. For some questions, I ended up revising my answers five times over those two weeks.

But as I worked through these applications, I gradually started to clarify my own thinking. The questions pushed me to consider things I'd never really thought about before. And after going through application after application, I realized how much my ideas had evolved. My biggest problem, I realized, was that I hadn't been thinking boldly enough or big enough. I'd been so focused on finding a way into a specific niche market that I'd never seriously considered how to turn that initial opportunity into something much bigger. What would it take to redefine an entire industry, one with a market worth hundreds of billions or even trillions of dollars? My way of thinking had always been more academic: identify a specific research problem and figure out how to solve it. But building a startup requires a completely different mindset. I feel like I'm only now truly making the shift from thinking like a researcher to thinking like a founder.

After these two weeks of intense reflection, I have a much clearer sense of what I want to build, what I hope to achieve ten years from now, and what I want the industry I'm trying to define to look like by then. Before, I simply felt that the world was changing incredibly fast, with every year, or even every week, looking different from the last. But now, instead of just watching those changes unfold, I have a much clearer, more ambitious vision of the future and a goal worth spending the next decade pursuing.
